## Optional pre-play supplement

This is additional teaching material based on the original **Yalta Game – Build Prompt**, not text from the supplied case study or an independently verified historical source. It explains terms already used by the game. It does not reveal which settlements were historically reached or award points. A facilitator can share it alongside the original case if students need every option explained before play.

### Japan: what is being traded?

The territorial option names southern Sakhalin, the Kuril Islands and privileges in Manchuria. Compare concessions affecting territory or rights with promises of economic aid and an appeal to the wartime alliance. Consider who would bear each concession, who is represented at the conference, and which promises Roosevelt has the authority to deliver.

### Poland: what would a safeguard do?

An ambassador reporting back provides information to a government; an observer supervising an election seeks a more direct checking role. Neither label by itself explains how access would be secured or what would happen after a violation. A threat to end cooperation also needs a credible way to carry it out. Distinguish an election promise, a way to check it and a response if it is broken.

### United Nations: which kind of power?

Keep discussing a dispute separate from taking a substantive decision about it. Also consider whether a permanent member that is itself involved in a dispute should vote when the Council addresses peaceful settlement. In the Assembly question, the game offers one Soviet vote, three votes through additional seats for Ukraine and Belarus, or sixteen republic seats. Assembly representation and Security Council veto power are different bargaining questions.

### Germany: decide what, and when?

Occupation zones determine who administers territory; they do not automatically create permanent new states. The game also asks students to compare fixing reparations at $20 billion, half to the USSR, with treating that figure as a basis for discussion and passing details to a committee or commission. A deferral preserves flexibility but gives later decision-makers influence over the final terms.
