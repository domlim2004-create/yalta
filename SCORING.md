# Facilitator scoring reference — spoilers

These are pedagogical assessments for the simulation, not historical facts. Scores are hidden in the normal interface until the ending, but are visible in this offline source and reference. Relationships are separate.

## Historical agreements

| Decision | Points | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| J1a | +2 | You secure a route to Soviet cooperation, while leaving its price to the next negotiation. |
| J2a | +1 | You obtain cooperation by conceding interests of parties absent from the table. |
| P1a | +1 | You obtain agreement using the existing balance of power, at a cost to representation. |
| P2a | +0 | A pledge closes the negotiation, but gives you little means to verify or enforce it. |
| U1a | +2 | You balance great-power participation with room for peaceful discussion. |
| U2a | +2 | A limited concession offers Stalin something he values without granting sixteen votes. |
| G1a | +2 | You share the occupation burden without taking territory from the Soviet zone. |
| G2a | +1 | Deferral saves scarce time but passes difficult decisions to later meetings. |

## Counteroffers

| Decision | Accept | Further bargaining | Reason for accepting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | +1 | +1 | You retain British support while returning to a deliverable military objective. |
| J2 | +1 | +1 | You obtain cooperation, but still concede interests not represented at the table. |
| P1 | +1 | +1 | You gain a seat for additional voices without changing the underlying balance of power. |
| P2 | +0 | +2 | You secure a pledge while accepting weak verification. |
| U1 | +2 | +2 | You preserve participation and a route to peaceful discussion. |
| U2 | +2 | +2 | A limited concession preserves participation without granting the full demand. |
| G1 | +2 | +2 | You share the burden through a concession the Western powers can deliver. |
| G2 | +1 | +1 | You preserve time by deferring questions, at the cost of control over their resolution. |

Further bargaining following an opening proposal rated −1 is capped at +1: recovery is recognised without erasing the unsupported demand. A failed roll’s free conditional settlement earns the historical proposal score minus one, floored at −1, because the group accepts weaker terms. Paying for further bargaining follows the table instead. No points are deducted solely for rolling badly.

Walking away after an opening proposal rated −1 earns −1; other counteroffer walkaways earn 0. Deferring directly before sending earns 0. Unattempted decisions contribute no points and remain separately labelled unresolved. Each decision is recorded once; Undo reverses that record.

## Opening-position assessments used for counteroffer resolution

| Proposal | Initial assessment | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| J1b: Ask Churchill for more British forces in the Far East | +0 | You keep Britain involved, but British support cannot replace the Soviet role you sought. |
| J1c: Ask no one: rely on the atomic bomb and a US invasion | -1 | You preserve independence but base your position on an untested weapon and an uncertain invasion. |
| J2b: Postwar economic aid for Soviet reconstruction | +0 | Aid remains an uncertain promise without authority to guarantee it. |
| J2c: Nothing extra: appeal to the alliance | -1 | An appeal to friendship gives Stalin little concrete reason to take on a new commitment. |
| P1b: Insist on the London government-in-exile | +1 | You defend representation but must recognise that an insistence alone cannot change control on the ground. |
| P1c: Scrap both and form a new government from scratch | +0 | A clean slate offers inclusion in principle but lacks a workable route through Soviet opposition. |
| P2b: Demand international observers to supervise the elections | +2 | You seek meaningful safeguards while recognising that access must be negotiated. |
| P2c: Threaten Stalin: free elections or no further cooperation | -1 | The threat risks cooperation without a credible means of enforcement. |
| U1b: No veto: majority vote decides everything | +0 | Equal voting is attractive, but participation is doubtful if the powers can simply leave. |
| U1c: An unlimited veto over everything, including blocking the Council from even discussing a dispute | -1 | An unrestricted veto protects members while undermining the organisation’s ability to discuss disputes. |
| U2b: Accept all 16 seats | -1 | Sixteen votes gives away disproportionate influence and strains British support. |
| U2c: Refuse any extra seats: one state, one vote | +1 | You defend voting equality but risk participation over a concession with limited practical cost. |
| G1b: Three powers only: France gets nothing | +0 | A simpler arrangement leaves Britain with less help in the longer term. |
| G1c: A French zone taken partly from the Soviet zone | -1 | You ask Stalin to surrender ground without a clear incentive for him to do so. |
| G2b: Split Germany permanently into separate states, decided now | +0 | A fixed settlement promises certainty before the parties have settled workable terms. |
| G2c: Fix reparations now at $20 billion, half to the USSR | +0 | A fixed sum promises clarity but leaves unresolved questions about burden and capacity to pay. |

These opening assessments are not additional points. Only the final settled outcome contributes to the total. Facilitators should review the rubric before a seminar and invite students to challenge its assumptions afterwards.
