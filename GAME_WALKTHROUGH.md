# Yalta 1945: Detailed Game Progression and Facilitator Instructions

This walkthrough explains how to run the game from its opening screen to its final discussion. The two worked examples at the end contain answers: use those for facilitator preparation rather than showing them before students play.

## 1. Prepare the session

Open the game on a laptop and, if useful, mirror it to a projector. Four or five students make decisions together as **one Roosevelt**. Stalin and Churchill are represented by the game's responses; students do not need to take those roles.

Choose one person to operate the controls. The group should discuss each choice before the operator selects it. You can suggest a spokesperson or rotate who explains the group's reasoning, but this is facilitation advice, not an additional game rule.

Allow approximately 30 minutes for play and additional time for the historical comparison and debrief. Time spent discussing does not automatically spend game days. Days are resources charged by particular actions, not a real-time countdown.

If a previous game loads, select **Restart → Start new game**. Decide whether to use the on-screen die or a real six-sided die. For a real die, enable **Use physical die**; enter the actual result when asked.

Facilitator notes are hidden by default. Press **F** to show them. On a mirrored projector, everyone can see the notes while they are open, including hints about issue order.

## 2. Begin and read the briefing

Click **Enter the briefing room**. Read the briefing aloud:

> You are Franklin Roosevelt. You are ill, and you have eight days. Four issues are on the table: the war against Japan, Poland, the United Nations and Germany. You cannot give everything equal attention. Which do you take first, and why?

Explain the starting position:

| Resource | Starting value | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days remaining | 8 | Time available for negotiations and relationship repair |
| Stalin relationship | 3 — Wary | Determines the target for Stalin rolls |
| Churchill relationship | 3 — Wary | Can block opening the UN if reduced to 1 |
| Completed decisions | 0 of 8 | Two decisions for each of four issues |

Both relationship tracks stay between 1 and 5: **Hostile, Cool, Wary, Warm, Close**.

Read the short rules panel, then select **Take your seat**.

## 3. Choose an issue

The conference table presents four dossiers: Japan, Poland, the United Nations and Germany. Ask the group to explain its first choice before clicking.

Useful prompts include:

- What do you hope to gain by settling this first?
- Whose cooperation will you need afterwards?
- What are you prepared to leave unresolved if time runs out?

Opening an available issue does not itself spend a day. It takes the group to that issue's first decision.

**Once an issue is opened, complete decision 1 and decision 2 before moving to another issue.** A dead end does not allow you to abandon the issue and switch tables. Completed issues cannot be selected again.

| Issue | Decision 1 | Decision 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japan | Who do you turn to? | What do you offer Stalin? |
| Poland | Which government do you build on? | How do you make sure the elections are free? |
| UN | How strong should the veto be? | How many Assembly seats for the USSR? |
| Germany | What role does France get? | Decide the hard questions now, or later? |

## 4. Discuss and select an option

Each decision presents a question and three options. Read all three, then let the group decide.

The options are shuffled at the beginning of a game. Their order stays fixed during that game. Consequently, “option 1” is not a reliable answer across different games; choose by reading the text.

One option leads to the historical agreement, called the **main line**. The other two are **dead ends**. These categories are not shown before the group chooses.

A main-line option is the game's progressing option. It is not necessarily the fairest or morally best proposal. The discussion should examine why it was achievable and what accepting it cost.

Select an option by clicking it or pressing **1**, **2** or **3**.

## 5. Open and resolve the telegram

A sealed envelope appears, showing the chosen proposal. Click **Open telegram**, or use Enter/Space.

**The cost is charged when the envelope is opened.** The game then follows one of three paths.

### A. An agreement without a roll

1. Pay the stated cost: normally one day.
2. Apply the stated relationship changes, keeping each track within 1–5.
3. Mark the decision complete on the issue board.
4. Read the telegram and any character reactions.
5. Select **Continue**.

Deferring Germany's hard questions, G2a, is the exception: its agreement costs **zero days**.

Character reactions are labelled **“Dramatised line, not a historical quote.”** Treat them as prompts for discussion, not primary-source quotations.

### B. A dead end

1. Lose one day.
2. Apply the option's relationship changes.
3. Read why the proposal failed and any character reactions.
4. Select **Continue**.
5. If days remain, return to the same question with the same options.

The decision remains incomplete. Relationship changes are not reversed when you return. Choosing the same dead end again spends another day and applies its changes again.

Ask: “Which assumption about your leverage was wrong?” Then let the group choose again.

### C. An agreement requiring a roll

Three agreement cards require a roll: **J2a, U1a and U2a**.

1. Pay the agreement's one-day cost.
2. Read the target shown on screen. It uses Stalin's score **before** the agreement's relationship changes.
3. Click **Roll die**, or roll the physical die and enter its result using a button from 1–6.
4. Resolve success or failure.

| Stalin score before the roll | Result needed | Chance of success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Hostile | 6 only | 1 in 6 |
| 2 — Cool | 5 or 6 | 2 in 6 |
| 3 — Wary | 4, 5 or 6 | 3 in 6 |
| 4 — Warm | 3, 4, 5 or 6 | 4 in 6 |
| 5 — Close | 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 | 5 in 6 |

On success, apply the agreement's relationship changes and mark the decision complete.

On failure, an extra day is due. If it can be paid, **X1 — Stalin stalls** appears:

> Molotov raises new conditions, and a day is lost in haggling. Then Stalin agrees.

Read X1, then select **Apply the agreement**. The normal relationship changes apply and the decision becomes complete. A failed roll therefore delays an agreement rather than sending you back to choose another option.

If the extra day cannot be paid, X3 ends the negotiations instead. The pending agreement is not completed and its relationship changes do not apply.

**Example:** With Stalin at 3 and six days left, choose J2a. Opening the envelope leaves five days. A roll of 3 fails; X1 leaves four days. Applying the agreement raises Stalin to 4 and completes J2. A roll of 4 would succeed, leaving five days and Stalin at 4.

## 6. Continue to the next decision or issue

After a completed decision, press **Continue**:

- After decision 1, proceed directly to decision 2 of the same issue.
- After decision 2, return to issue selection if other issues remain and there is time.
- After the eighth completed decision, go to the completed-game ending.
- If another decision is needed but no days remain, show X3 and end negotiations.

The issue board has two markers per issue. A box represents a completed decision, not an attempt. Selecting an option or spending a day on a dead end does not tick it.

## 7. Repair relationships with a banquet toast

A toast is available on the issue-selection or decision screen, before selecting an option. It is unavailable while opening or resolving a telegram or roll.

1. Click **Banquet toast**.
2. Choose Stalin or Churchill.
3. Spend one day and raise that leader's score by one.
4. Return to the negotiation screen, provided days remain.

Opening the toast menu costs nothing; cancelling costs nothing. A leader already at 5 cannot receive a further increase. You cannot toast at zero days.

A toast improves the relationship but does not complete a decision. Spending your last day on a toast immediately ends negotiations with X3. Repairing a relationship on the last day therefore does not leave time to use that improvement.

## 8. Handle a locked UN

If either Stalin or Churchill is at 1, the UN shows a lock. Attempting to open it displays **X2 — UN locked**:

> Relations are too strained to plan a world organisation. Spend days on banquet toasts until Stalin and Churchill are both at 2 or higher, or choose another issue.

This attempt costs nothing and changes no relationship scores. Select **Return to the table**.

To open the UN, both leaders must be at least 2. Toast the hostile leader, or complete another issue whose effects improve the needed relationship. If both leaders are at 1, each needs repair.

The lock is checked when opening the UN. It does not eject the group from UN negotiations already underway if a later dead end reduces a relationship to 1.

## 9. Understand exactly when time runs out

There are two ways to end negotiations:

**All eight decisions are completed.** The ending displays:

> You completed every issue before leaving Yalta.

This completion check takes priority over the zero-day check. Completing the final decision with exactly zero days left is allowed.

**Time prevents further progress.** X3 appears when a required cost cannot be paid, or when no days remain and the group needs to start another decision or retry a dead end:

> Exhausted and ill, Roosevelt must go home. Every unresolved issue stays open, and the next conference will be held without him.

If you can pay a current agreement's entire cost, it can complete even if it leaves zero days. You then leave before starting the next decision. If a roll fails after the base cost used the final day, its extra cost cannot be paid and that agreement stays incomplete.

Germany's zero-cost agreement is not an exception to the requirement to have time when beginning a new decision. You cannot reach zero days on G1 and then start G2 for free.

Days never become negative. Already completed decisions remain completed.

## 10. Read the group's ending

The outcome screen lists completed decisions, unresolved issues, final relationship scores and days remaining.

Before revealing history, ask players to explain their own results:

- Which issues did you finish?
- Which did you leave unresolved or never open?
- Where did your spare days go?
- Which relationship limited your choices?

An incomplete conference is a valid learning outcome. Avoid treating the ending as a simple pass/fail grade for the students.

## 11. Reveal history one row at a time

Select **What happened in history**. The historical comparison reveals Japan, Poland, the UN and Germany in that order, regardless of the group's play order.

Click the reveal button once for each row. Compare the historical agreement and its aftermath with the group's status:

| Group status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Main line reached | Both decisions on this issue were completed |
| Not reached | The issue was opened, but one or both decisions remain incomplete |
| Never opened | The group never began the issue; an unsuccessful locked-UN attempt does not count as opening it |

After the fourth row, read the closing paragraph. Then select **Begin the debrief**.

The comparison should help students separate securing an agreement from ensuring its enforcement or long-term success.

## 12. Debrief and print the record

Use the supplied questions under **Setup (3-D)**, **Deal design (2-D)**, **Tactics (1-D)** and **Big picture**. Choose how much time to spend on each according to your seminar schedule.

Start with specific choices from this group's decision log. Discuss issue order, goodwill, absent parties, vague wording, deferred decisions and the difference between bad choices and bad luck.

Finish with the supplied question:

> Was Yalta a sellout, or the best deal Roosevelt's position allowed?

Click **Print group summary** to open the browser's print dialog. Print or save a PDF containing the debrief, outcome and choice/card/roll log.

## 13. Controls, Undo and saving

| Control | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 / 2 / 3 | Choose the corresponding option on a decision screen |
| Enter / Space | Advance a screen; activate a focused button using its normal keyboard behaviour |
| Tab / Shift+Tab | Move between interactive controls |
| F | Show or hide facilitator notes |
| U / Undo button | Reverse the last game-state action |
| Restart | Open the confirmation for a fresh game with newly shuffled options |
| Decision log | Expand the record of choices, cards and rolls |

Undo operates one action at a time. For example, undoing Continue restores the preceding card; another Undo may reverse its resolution. Rolls, days, relationships and log entries are restored with the relevant state. It can also cancel an animated roll. Use it to correct an accidental input; allowing strategic rewinds is a facilitator choice, not a requirement.

The game saves progress locally when storage is available. Refresh normally restores it. If browser storage is unavailable, the game remains playable but cannot preserve progress through a refresh. Use Restart for a fresh group rather than relying on refresh.

## 14. Facilitator example: complete all issues

**Spoilers:** This is a demonstration route, not a sequence to announce to players. Choose the main-line option each time and assume every die roll succeeds.

| Step | Action | Days left | Stalin | Churchill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Begin conference | 8 | 3 | 3 |
| 1 | Japan J1a: ask Stalin | 7 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | Japan J2a: offer territory and rights; roll 4+ | 6 | 4 | 3 |
| 3 | UN U1a: veto with limits; roll 3+ | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| 4 | UN U2a: two extra seats; roll 3+ | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| 5 | Germany G1a: French zone and Council seat | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| 6 | Germany G2a: defer the hard questions | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| 7 | Poland P1a: broaden the Soviet-backed government | 2 | 5 | 3 |
| 8 | Poland P2a: accept the election pledge | 1 | 5 | 2 |

The game ends with all eight decisions complete and one day left. Discuss why completing the board does not establish that all agreements were fair or enforceable.

## 15. Facilitator example: setbacks and an incomplete ending

This rehearsal deliberately uses setbacks, including repeating a dead end. It checks that the facilitator understands the consequences rather than illustrating recommended strategy.

| Step | Action | Days left | Stalin | Churchill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Begin conference | 8 | 3 | 3 |
| 1 | Japan J1b: ask Churchill; dead end | 7 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | Retry J1 with J1a | 6 | 3 | 3 |
| 3 | J2a, roll 3: base cost and X1 extra cost; then apply agreement | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| 4 | Germany G1c: take land from Soviet zone; dead end | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 5 | Repeat G1c; another dead end | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| 6 | G1a: agree the French role | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 7 | G2a: defer hard questions | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 8 | Attempt to open UN: X2 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 9 | Return to table; toast Stalin | 0 | 2 | 4 |

The toast repairs Stalin's relationship but spends the final day. X3 appears. Japan and Germany are complete; Poland and the UN remain unresolved. The UN was never opened because the lock prevented entry.

Ask students where they lost time, whether a different order could have helped, and whether the failed roll was a poor choice or an uncertain outcome.
