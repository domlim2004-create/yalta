# Detailed progression · Party-style redesign

This guide applies to `redesign/party-style`. The earlier forced-main-line game is preserved at `pre-party-redesign`.

## 1. Start a separate conference

Open `index.html` and choose **Take your seat**, then read the briefing and scoring rubric. The original game's save is not imported or overwritten. If a redesign game was saved, it resumes; use Restart for a new group.

Start with eight days, Stalin 3 and Churchill 3. There is no real-time timer. Talking does not spend days.

## 2. Pick an issue at the table

Churchill and Stalin face your Roosevelt seat across the table, with four coloured dossiers. Open Japan, Poland, the UN or Germany. Opening costs nothing. A short hand-swipe clears the other cards: two hands for a middle card, one forearm for an end card. Escape or U cancels the sweep; reduced-motion settings skip it. If either leader is at 1, opening the UN is blocked without a cost. Return and repair relations or choose another issue.

Complete decision 1 then decision 2 before returning to the table, or deliberately leave the issue unresolved. You cannot reopen a closed issue.

## 3. Select, send, hear reactions and confirm

Each decision shows three shuffled proposals. Their order remains fixed within a game.

The page shows the situation followed by three proposal cards. Selecting a card opens its discussion panel. Selecting an option opens a full-width panel below the cards with two numbered thinking questions, a short guide and a reference to the relevant case-study sections. The other proposals remain available; switching cards changes the questions. Discuss aloud, then use **Send proposal**. No written answer is required and no score is shown.

Send opens a separate **Read the room** page with two restrained speech bubbles from Stalin, Churchill or a fictional adviser. All are labelled as dramatised. These first impressions are not an acceptance or final refusal, and show no score or numerical relationship effect. Interpret them as a group rather than trying to identify an obvious approval signal.

Choose **Stand by this proposal** to commit its displayed day cost, or **Reconsider** to return to the decision for free. Escape also reconsiders. Merely selecting or sending tentatively changes no days, relationships, scores, results or action log. The pre-send questions remain available in optional facilitator notes on this page.

Most confirmed proposals cost one day. Referring Germany's hard questions costs zero, but beginning that decision still requires time remaining. Only after confirmation does the game reveal the actual agreement, die request or counteroffer.

## 4. Receive a response

A historical agreement without a roll immediately records its outcome and changes relationships. Read its supplied text and any dramatised reactions, then Continue.

After confirmation, an alternative proposal receives a simulated counteroffer. Its initial relationship effects apply immediately and are reported. The original proposal's day is already spent. It does not send you back to guess another answer.

## 5. Resolve a roll if required

Historical agreement proposals J2a, U1a and U2a require a roll after the base day is paid but before agreement relationship changes.

| Stalin | Target |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 6 |
| 2 | 5+ |
| 3 | 4+ |
| 4 | 3+ |
| 5 | 2+ |

Use the on-screen die or enable Physical die and enter the actual result. The on-screen die comes in from below/right, turns twice, then stops on its result; read the number on the top face and press **Continue**. Reduced-motion preferences show the settled result immediately. A settled digital result survives refresh. Physical-die entry resolves directly. Success records the historical agreement. Failure creates a simulated complication without automatically charging another day. No score is assigned merely for the roll.

## 6. Choose a response to the counteroffer

Every counteroffer shows its proposed terms in a speech bubble beside the seated speaker, with three responses on the table. Agreement reactions use the same seated speech bubbles:

- **Accept:** no additional day. After a failed roll, this accepts conditional support with details left open. Otherwise it accepts the stated compromise.
- **Negotiate the named improvement:** costs one extra day, or zero when the responding leader is at least 4. The button describes the outcome and relationship changes. It is disabled if unaffordable. This is a specified limited improvement, not a guarantee that the original demand succeeds.
- **Walk away:** no additional day or relationship change. The current decision receives an explained walkaway score; the issue closes, and its remaining decision is unattempted.

The selected response settles the decision exactly once. Only its final outcome contributes points. Initial and settlement relationship changes can both apply; all tracks clamp to 1–5.

## 7. Continue or repair relationships

After an agreement, Continue advances to decision 2 or returns to issue selection. After a walkaway, Continue returns to the table if time and other issues remain.

On issue selection or a decision screen, **Toast · 1 day** opens a menu. Choose a leader to raise their score by one. Opening or cancelling the menu is free. A leader at 5 cannot receive another increase. Spending the final day on a toast immediately ends the conference.

On a decision screen, **Leave this issue unresolved** asks for confirmation. This records a zero-point deliberate deferral for the current decision; remaining decisions stay unattempted. On issue selection, **Close conference early** ends play after confirmation.

## 8. Understand exhaustion

At zero days you cannot start another decision. However, a counteroffer already in progress still allows free acceptance, affordable bargaining or a walkaway. This ensures the group can respond to a complication even after paying its last base day.

After that decision settles, Continue ends play if further decisions would need to begin. Days never become negative. Finishing all issues on exactly zero days is allowed. Leaving an issue does not mark its decisions as agreements.

## 9. Reveal the outcome

The ending shows decision points, agreements out of eight, unresolved decisions, days and final relationships. Every settled decision displays the proposal, final terms, its integer score and an explanation. Unattempted decisions are labelled unresolved without a fabricated choice or score.

The rubric is:

- +2: strong outcome within available leverage and constraints.
- +1: defensible compromise with costs.
- 0: limited progress or broadly balanced costs and benefits.
- −1: a costly, unsupported outcome.

The theoretical total ranges from −8 to +16, but there is no pass mark and no claim that every combination or maximum is reachable. Unattempted decisions add nothing. Compare the total with coverage: closing immediately is zero points and zero agreements, not a successful conference.

## 10. Compare history and discuss

Reveal Japan, Poland, the UN and Germany one row at a time. The historical text is unchanged from the supplied prompt. Your simulated outcome is reported separately, with the number of agreements on each issue. Alternative agreements are not presented as things that actually occurred.

After the closing paragraph, continue to the debrief. The original ten questions remain, with an invitation to challenge scores using the group's reasoning. Back to home returns to the title page with a fresh conference. Undo can restore the completed conference.

## 11. Example routes (facilitator spoilers)

**Historical route:** Japan → UN → Germany → Poland, selecting historical proposals with successful rolls, completes eight agreements with one day left, Stalin 5 and Churchill 2. It earns 11 points under this teaching rubric; historical choices are not uniformly +2.

**Adverse route:** J1a → J2c, then further bargaining → G1c, then accept the counteroffer → G2a. Stalin reaches 1 with four days left. Attempt UN: blocked at no cost. Toast Stalin to 2, then open UN. Send and stand by U1a, then fail the roll; accept conditional support. Send and stand by U2a, then fail; accept conditional support. One day remains. A final toast ends the conference, leaving Poland unattempted. Both failures offered choices rather than automatic extra-day penalties.

**Deliberate walkaway:** Open Poland, choose a proposal and hear its counteroffer. Walk away if its terms cannot be defended. The issue closes; P2 is unattempted. Remaining time can be used on another issue. The ending explains the scored P1 outcome without treating a walkaway as an agreement.

## 12. Controls

1–3 selects proposals. Tab moves focus; Enter/Space activates buttons. Enter/Space on a Continue screen advances. Sending and standing by a proposal each require explicit button actions. Escape on the reaction page reconsiders and returns focus to the option. F toggles facilitator notes; U reverses the last game action. Undo restores hidden scores, relationships, days, orders and logs together and cancels pending roll animation. Restart is confirmed and reversible.

The redesigned save key is separate from the original. Browser storage failure does not prevent play, but progress may be lost on refresh. The original eight-day version and its documentation remain recoverable through the backup tag.

The introduction is split into two pages: mission and constraints first, then five short play rules. Scoring details are optional on the rules page. Back returns to the mission; neither page spends days.
